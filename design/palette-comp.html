Palette exploration

Two things came out of this, and the second one matters more. Measured: the palette's problem is not which colours it uses, it is how many jobs colour is being asked to do — seven chromatic roles on one screen, six of them crowded into a 39° hue arc, and no guard anywhere between the three separate colour scales that render together. Proposed: move emphasis onto border weight, retire three hues outright, then choose a ground.

design/palette-comp.html — open directly, not app code. Every specimen below renders identical markup, so the only variable is the token block.

01 · MEASURE

What the current palette does wrong

Six tokens live inside a 39° hue arc, all at 47–63% lightness. Measured from index.css:

Collision 1 — the accent is inside the thermal ramp. --copper (28°, L 54%) and --t-warm (34°, L 61%) are 6° apart. An armed button and a warming heater are the same colour. --copper-bright (30°) sits between t-hot and t-warm. So the one colour meaning "you may touch this" is also the colour meaning "this is heating up".

Collision 2 — cold is a label. --t-cold (209°, L 55%) vs --silk-dim (211°, L 64%). A cold heater's numeral reads as disabled text.

Each scale polices itself and none of them look outward. Running the project's own deltaE across the seams finds 20 pairs under the codebase's own MIN_SEPARATION of 25. The top of the list:

ΔE 0.0--gold vs BED_COLOR Same hex, #C9A227, declared twice. Chrome means "warn / unhomed / standby / unsaved"; the chart means "this line is the bed". Both appear in the Temperatures card at once.

Two consequences. First, a collision cannot be fixed by moving the ramp — those three values are defaults the operator overwrites, so a palette whose separation depends on t-warm staying #E0A458 is separated by luck. Second, and this is the durable one: the repo already owns the arithmetic (util/colorDistance.ts) and already treats separation as an invariant for chart lines. It is simply never fed the chrome tokens. Whichever ground wins, feeding nearestCollision the chrome palette makes the thermal picker warn when an operator walks the ramp onto the accent — today it says nothing.

03 · PROPOSE

Colour is carrying weight that lines should carry

In the heater table the state is already written out — Active, Standby, Off, Fault — and then said a second time in four different hues. The colour is a duplicate of the label beside it. Four hues to encode what four words already encode is where the busy-ness comes from, and it is not fixed by choosing nicer hues. So emphasis moves onto border weight, and the colours that were only ever repeating themselves retire.

05 · CORRECT

Where colour is not redundant

My first cut drew Active / Standby / Off as three exclusive modes with one label each. That is not what they are, and the mistake was reading the pass-1 comp instead of ToolsHeatersCard.tsx. The real control:

  • Active and Standby are not mutually exclusive. Each owns its own setpoint field, and a heater holds both numbers at once. Only the mode is exclusive.
  • Each key is two presses. Press 1 writes that key's setpoint and arms the key; press 2 switches the heater to that mode. No single press can heat the machine.
  • There is no State column — it was removed precisely because the lit key already says it. My specimen had re-added the thing the app deleted.

So a key carries five independent facts at once, and my one-hue ladder had room for one of them:

FactChannelWhy not a shared one
Which key is thislabel + fixed column Act / Stand / Off never swap places. Today this is also carried by a permanent rest hue — the one signal the reduction can take back, since position already says it.
Armed — next press switches mode2 px border in the key's hue The middle of a two-press sequence must not look like having done nothing.
Engaged — the machine is in this modesolid fill Fill is the only channel loud enough to be read across the room.
Arrival — reading reached this key's setpointhalo, thermally coloured Sits on top of engaged so the two read independently; goes red past OVER_HOT_C.
Pending — field holds an unwritten valuecorner dot Rides the corner so a pending key is exactly as wide as a settled one.

There is also a written constraint here. control/setpointCommit.ts allows the emitted G-code to depend on state — normally forbidden here — on one condition, stated in the file: "`armed` drives the key's own colour, so the key always shows which of the two it will send before you press it. If that display is ever dropped, this multiplexing has to go with it."

Read it precisely: it requires a display, not a hue. Collapsing every key to one shared colour drops the display and would make the two-press behaviour illegal. Moving armed onto a thicker line does not — it makes the distinction louder than the alpha step it replaces. So the reduction does reach the keys; it just cannot reach them by deleting the signal.

CurrentProposed
resteach key in its own hue at 35% / 78% α — three chromatic buttons per row, permanently Neutral. An idle row has no chroma in it. Identity comes from the label and the fixed column, neither of which moves.
armedsame hue, alpha steps up — the quietest change in the sequence is the one that matters most 2 px in the key's own hue. Unmistakable against a neutral rest, and the hue still previews the mode the next press switches to.
engagedSolid fill in the key's hue — unchanged, and now the only filled thing in the row.
halo / dotUnchanged. Arrival is thermal data; the dot is an unwritten value.

Net effect on a five-heater card: 15 chromatic buttons at rest becomes 0, with hue appearing only on the one key per row that is engaged, plus any key you have armed. Colour stops meaning "this is a button" and starts meaning "this is happening" — which is the same rule as the rest of the pass, and app.css:2040's existing colour-and-shadow-only discipline already permits it.

Read across: hue is identity, fill is mode, halo is temperature, dot is an unwritten value. Four channels, one hue each — which is the same principle as the reduction, applied to a control that genuinely has four things to say instead of one thing said four times.

08 · CHOOSE

Where I'd land

The ladder regardless of the ground. It is the part that answers the actual complaint, it is orthogonal to which palette wins, and it takes three hues out of the app on its own.

Then ground 1 or 2. Quiet navy costs nothing and keeps the identity; quiet anodize is the only one that closes both collisions by construction rather than by taste. Ground 3 is the compromise between them, ground 4 is the interesting risk, ground 5 is a later theme.

And the guard, either way. Every number on this page came from util/colorDistance.ts, which the repo already trusts enough to hang a rung-8 invariant on — it has simply never been pointed at the chrome. Feeding nearestCollision the chrome tokens costs one argument at two call sites and turns "these colours are separated" from taste into something CI can fail on. That is the part that stops the next collision rather than this one.
